What We Ask Before a Search Begins

One of the first questions we ask a hiring manager is,

“What would you like this person to take off your team’s plate?”

The answer often tells us more than the job title. Two employers hiring a project manager, for example, may need very different people. One needs someone who can turn an idea into a working plan. The other has the plan and needs someone who can keep three departments on schedule.

From there, we talk through what the new hire will own, who they’ll work with, and which skills they’ll need on day one. Those details give us better questions to ask candidates and give job seekers a clearer picture of the role before they interview.

Our Principles

Listen first.

Every search starts with the people who know the work. We ask hiring managers what their team needs and give job seekers room to share what they want from their next role.

Be clear about the opportunity.

We believe people make better decisions when they know what to expect. That means speaking plainly about the responsibilities, the work environment, and the next step in the hiring process.

Recognize skills wherever they were built.

A job title tells only part of someone’s story. We look at the problems a person has solved, the responsibilities they’ve earned, and how that experience could serve a new team.

What Makes Us Different

We build each search around a practical question:

What should this person be able to do in their first month?

For a manufacturer adding a second shift, the answer might be to troubleshoot equipment independently. For a finance team approaching year-end, it might be to reconcile accounts and explain discrepancies before the books close.

Those details shape whom we look for, what we ask in interviews, and how we introduce a candidate to your team. You can see why someone is worth meeting, and the candidate can picture the work they’re signing up for. That’s the approach we bring to employers across the United States hiring in healthcare, skilled industrial, engineering, IT, and finance.
